We took a deep dive into accessible names and descriptions in the previous chapters, and we’ve learned about the importance of accessible names to assistive technology users like screen reader users.

We’ve also talked about labels and how labels are not the same as accessible names. Though, **ideally, they should be identical.** In this chapter, we’re going to understand why.

And we’re going to learn why **missing** or **repeated text labels** can cause barriers to access to some assistive technology users.

More specifically, we’re going to learn about speech recognition software, how it works, how people use it to navigate and operate web content, and how the design decisions we make can improve or degrade their user experience, and what to do instead.

So, let’s dive right in.
